# SFX Type Library

Every sound type, what it does, and the exact English phrasing to drop into a prompt.

**Describe physical behaviour, never a library name.**
- ❌ `add a whoosh SFX`
- ✅ `an airy whoosh with real doppler, rising and falling with the camera arc, short tail`

---

## IMPACT FAMILY — sounds that stop time

### Impact
Full-spectrum collision. Sub + body + top transient, three layers from three sources.
Use for: collisions, landings, things breaking, the end of a build.
> `a deep full-spectrum impact with sub content down to 30Hz, layered with a metallic body and a sharp top transient, carrying a long reverberant tail`

### Hit
Lighter and shorter than an impact. Clean, no long tail.
Use for: cuts, logo reveals, quick visual confirmations, motion graphics.
Difference: a hit serves rhythm, an impact serves drama.
> `a clean short hit with a tight transient and a short warm tail, no sub bloom`

### Boom
Low-frequency explosion, energy under 100Hz.
Use for: the peak moment, the big reveal, the last frame.
**Limit: one or two per film. Never more.**
> `a deep boom dropping to 30Hz with a slow decaying tail`

### Braaam
Long distorted brass swell.
Use for: trailers, epic beats.
**Burnt-out cliché. Avoid in anything premium, restrained or elegant.**
> `a low distorted brass braam swelling and decaying over two seconds`

### Stinger
Short sharp musical-flavoured hit, usually strings or metal.
Use for: horror jump beats, sudden reveals, dramatic punctuation.
> `a sharp metallic stinger with a fast attack and a ringing tail`

### Sub Drop
Descending low frequency. Felt in the chest, not heard.
Use for: underneath any large hit, or alone entering a heavier scene.
> `a sub-bass drop sweeping from 80Hz down to 25Hz over half a second`

## MOTION FAMILY — sounds that move the ear

### Whoosh
Moving air. The most-used element in modern editing.

| Variant | Character | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Slow whoosh | rises and falls with the move | long drifts, arcs, slow pushes |
| Fast whoosh | short tail, tight | whip pans, fast zooms, snaps |
| Doppler whoosh | real pitch shift | anything passing the camera |
| Textured whoosh | grain instead of clean air | sand, metal, water, fire worlds |

**Law: a whoosh starts 3–5 frames BEFORE the move and ends with it.** Starting on the move reads as late.
> `an airy whoosh with real doppler movement across the stereo field, eased in and out with the camera move, short tail`

### Transition
A complete element covering a cut. Classic build: reverse swell → whoosh → hit → tail.
> `a reverse swell rising into the cut, a whoosh across it, and a soft hit landing on the cut frame`

### Movement / Mechanical
Gears, joints, servos, sliding metal.
Use for: robotics, future interfaces, machinery, tech product reveals.
> `precise servo movement with fine mechanical detail and a metallic settle`

### Slow Motion
Time-stretched material — lowered pitch, long tails, detail pulled apart.
Use for: any slow-motion shot. Without it slow motion feels dead.
> `time-stretched airy texture with lowered pitch and long smeared tails`

## BUILD & TENSION FAMILY

### Riser / Uplifter
Climbs in pitch, density, or both, and resolves on a hit.
**Iron law: every riser must land on something.** A riser with no target is amateur.
Variants: tonal riser · noise riser · rhythmic riser (accelerating pulses).
> `a rising uplifter climbing steadily over two seconds and resolving into the impact at 4.0s`

### Downer / Negative Transition
The inverse. Descends.
Use for: after the peak, entering a sadder scene, the ending.
> `a descending downer easing the energy out of the scene`

### Reverse / Pre-hit
Reversed sound growing inward, cutting dead on the hit.
Place 0.3–0.8s before every important hit. This is what makes an impact feel intentional rather than accidental.
> `a reverse swell growing over 0.5s and cutting dead on the hit frame`

### Drone
Continuous, non-rhythmic bed carrying tension or space.
Variants: dark (threat) · airy (scale) · tonal (stability) · dissonant (unease).
> `a continuous low drone, thick and slow-moving, thickening in density across the shot`

### Tension / Texture
Layers that don't read as identifiable sounds — friction, scrape, ring, grain.
Use under quiet scenes so they don't feel empty.
> `fine granular texture and filtered noise sitting under the frame, felt more than heard`

## BED FAMILY

### Ambience / Background
The sound of the place itself, continuous and stable.
Types: urban/city · nature · jungle/rainforest · ocean/waves · countryside/rural/farm · interior.
**Law: ambience changes on the exact frame the location changes.** Carrying one bed across a location cut reads as wrong even when the viewer can't say why.
> `a wide open outdoor ambience — soft air, distant crowd murmur held far back, light breeze in trees`

### Room Tone
The real silence of a room. Not zero — silence with colour.
Absolute digital silence reads as dead and broken. There is always room tone.
> `near-silent clean room tone with a faint low warm bed under it`

### Water
Its own category: waves, flow, drips, submersion, bubbles.
> `water with real body and weight, close droplets over a distant flowing bed`

## FOLEY FAMILY

Three standard classes:
1. **Footsteps** — every surface is a different sound (wood, concrete, gravel, carpet, metal, snow).
2. **Cloth / Movement** — fabric friction on every body movement.
3. **Props / Specifics** — anything handled, placed, opened.

**Professional law: record close and dry, add the room reverb as a separate layer afterwards.** Never the reverse.
> `close, dry, high-resolution foley — fabric shifts, weight transfers, footfalls with real floor material under them`

### Camera SFX
Shutter, focus, zoom, motor. Use in films about filming, or as a photographic transition device.

### Utility / Workspace
Keyboard, mouse, paper, chair, cup.
> `individual key presses, dry and soft, spaced to the real hand movement, with a small chair creak`

## UI / TECH FAMILY

| Type | Character | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Click / Tap | short, clean transient | pressing a control |
| Notification | short pleasant tone, often two notes | message, alert |
| Bleep | simple digital tone | confirm, reject |
| Glitch | digital tearing, grain, error | malfunction, cyber style |
| Alarm | repeating, high, uncomfortable | warning, danger |
| UI whoosh | small clean whoosh | panels opening and closing |
| Sci-fi UI | the same types with a future layer | holograms, fictional interfaces |

**The premium-UI secret:** a felt-padded click over a warm rounded low tone with a fast air tail. No crack, no hard transient. This is the entire difference between an Apple sound and a free-app sound.
> `a felt-padded click over a warm rounded low tone with a fast air tail, no crack, no hard transient`

## TONAL & MUSICAL FAMILY

### Rhythmic / Percussion
Pulses that carry pace without becoming full music. Ideal for ads and motion graphics.

### Strings
From long and mournful to nervous staccato. The strongest emotional tool available.

### Piano Pings / Felt Piano
Single soft piano notes. Currently the default sound of premium tech and luxury advertising.

### Tonal SFX
Effects with a defined pitch, so the design sits with the music instead of fighting it.
**Pro move:** find the key of the music and pick tonal elements from the same key.
