# Palettes, Timing Laws, Mix

---

## GENRE PALETTES

Pick one. Mixing palettes is what makes a sound bed feel confused.

### Cinematic / Trailer
`impacts + booms + risers + reverses + drones + strings`
Three-part arc: build → silence → hit. Repeat, escalating each time.
Skip braaams unless the client explicitly wants that 2010s trailer sound.

### Sci-Fi
`mechanical + servo + glitches + tonal drones + UI bleeps + energy textures`
Everything obeys a technical logic. Nothing organic.

### Horror
`dissonant drones + stingers + reverses + textures + silence`
Silence is the weapon. Horror is built out of emptiness, not noise.

### User Interface / Tech / Product
`soft clicks + notifications + light whooshes + clean room tone + minimal pad`
Clean, precise, quiet. No big impacts, no risers.

### Motion Graphics
`hits + short whooshes + rhythmic pulses + tonal accents`
Every visual move gets a sound. Rhythm matters more than realism.

### Sports
`impacts + breath + fabric + crowd + rhythmic builds`
Physical energy lives in the detail: breath, friction, contact.

### Luxury / Premium
`minimal foley + air + one soft pad + real silence`
Less is the whole point. Expensive sounds like confident quiet, not noise.

## TIMING LAWS

1. **Whooshes start 3–5 frames before the move.**
2. **Hits land on the exact frame** — not before, not after.
3. **A reverse precedes every important hit by 0.3–0.8s.**
4. **Put 0.15–0.25s of silence before the biggest hit.** The single most powerful move in sound design.
5. **Ambience switches on the location-cut frame** — not before, not after.
6. **One transition element per cut.** Stacking cancels the effect.
7. **A repeated element dies.** Use a boom twice and the third one is invisible.
8. **Distance in frame = distance in the mix.** Near is dry and forward; far is reverberant and back.
9. **Nothing gets a sound for movement that isn't on screen.** If the subject is frozen, all motion sound belongs to the camera and the air.
10. **Scale the weight to the picture.** The heaviest hit belongs to the heaviest visual event, and everything else sits under it.

## MIX

### Frequency allocation
| Range | Owner |
|---|---|
| 20–60Hz | sub, booms |
| 60–200Hz | impact body, warmth |
| 200Hz–1kHz | foley, physical body |
| 1–5kHz | clarity, transients |
| 5–16kHz | air, shimmer, the "expensive" band |

### Stereo placement
Foley centred · ambience wide · whooshes on the sides · impacts centre and wide together.

### Dynamics
Keep real distance between the quietest and loudest moments. A compressed mix sounds cheap even when the individual sounds are expensive. Let near-silence do work.

### Master for social
Around **-14 LUFS** integrated, **true peak under -1 dBTP**, stereo, frame-accurate.

## MISTAKES THAT KILL THE WORK

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| A whoosh on every cut | whooshes only where the camera actually moves |
| A riser with no target | every riser resolves on a hit |
| Booms everywhere | one or two per film |
| Absolute digital silence | there is always room tone |
| One ambience across a location cut | switch it on the cut frame |
| Foley drowned in reverb | record dry, add room as a separate layer |
| Sound design sitting on top of music | music underneath, design above it |
| Sound for movement not on screen | every sound needs a visible cause |
| Everything at one level | build a pyramid: floor, middle, peak |
| Stacked hits | one per moment |
